# Energy System Ontology (ESO)

The Energy System Ontology (ESO) is a structured knowledge model designed to describe the main concepts, relationships, and dynamics of energy systems in a consistent and interoperable way. It provides a shared vocabulary that supports the integration of heterogeneous data, the comparison of different studies, and the formulation of complex queries related to the energy transition. By organizing knowledge across domains such as technologies, policies, actors, and environmental factors, ESO enables a more systematic representation of how energy systems evolve and interact.

Within ESO, Ontology Design Patterns (ODPs) are reusable modeling solutions that capture recurring structures in the energy domain. They serve as modular building blocks that can be combined to represent specific aspects of the system, such as demand drivers, policy impacts, or technology adoption mechanisms. Each pattern encodes a well-defined conceptual structure, helping users apply the ontology consistently while reducing modeling effort and ambiguity. Together, these ODPs make ESO flexible, extensible, and easier to adopt in different analytical and application contexts.

This website provides access to the 18 ODPs defined in the ESO ontological framework.
